US patent maintenance fees: 2025 amounts, due dates & how they work

Last updated September 2026 · ~6 min read

A granted US patent isn't a one-time purchase — you have to pay the USPTO three maintenance fees over its life to keep it in force. Miss one and the patent lapses. Here's exactly what they cost in 2025, when they're due, and how to make sure you never miss a payment.

When are maintenance fees due?

US utility patents carry three maintenance fees, due at set points measured from the grant date (not the filing date):

  • 1st fee — 3.5 years after grant
  • 2nd fee — 7.5 years after grant
  • 3rd fee — 11.5 years after grant

Each fee can be paid, without any surcharge, in the six months before the due date. If you miss it, there's a further six-month grace period after the due date during which you can still pay — but with a late surcharge. After the grace period ends, the patent expires. (Design patents and plant patents don't carry maintenance fees — this applies to utility patents.)

2025 maintenance-fee amounts

The USPTO fee amounts below took effect on 19 January 2025. What you pay depends on your entity size: large (undiscounted), small entity (40% of the large fee), or micro entity (20%).

Which entity size are you?

  • Large / undiscounted — most companies, and anyone who has licensed the patent to a large entity.
  • Small entity — independent inventors, small businesses (generally <500 employees) and non-profits. Pays 40%.
  • Micro entity — small entities that also meet an income limit and a cap on prior filings. Pays 20%.

Entity status can change over the life of a patent (for example, if you license to a large company), and paying the wrong amount can create problems later — so it's worth confirming your status before each payment.

What happens if you miss a maintenance fee?

Once the six-month grace period ends, the patent lapses — it's no longer enforceable, and competitors are free to practise the invention. In some cases a lapsed patent can be revived by petition if the delay was "unintentional," with additional fees, but revival is never guaranteed and gets harder the longer you wait.

The painful part is the maths: the reminder that would have saved the patent costs nothing, while the right it protects can be worth orders of magnitude more. This is why missed maintenance fees are one of the most common — and most avoidable — ways a valuable patent dies.

How to never miss one

  1. Know your three dates for every granted patent — 3.5, 7.5 and 11.5 years from grant.
  2. Diarise the window, not just the date — you can pay in the six months before, so set a reminder ~6 months out.
  3. Confirm entity size before each payment.
  4. Track the whole portfolio in one place so a single case can't slip through — especially if different attorneys or firms handle different patents.
