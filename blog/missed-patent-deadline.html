What happens if you miss a patent deadline?

Last updated September 2026 · ~5 min read

Patent deadlines are unforgiving, but not always fatal. Whether a miss is a survivable slip or the end of the right depends on which deadline it was — and how fast you act. Here's what actually happens, and how to make sure it never gets that far.

Not all deadlines are equal

The three deadlines that most often get missed each behave differently:

  • Office-action responses (during examination) — a US response has a shortened statutory period, usually 3 months, extendable to 6 months with extension fees. Miss the maximum and the application goes abandoned.
  • Maintenance / renewal fees (after grant) — US maintenance fees have a 6-month grace period with a surcharge; European renewal fees have a 6-month grace period with a 50% additional fee. Miss the grace period and the patent lapses.
  • National / regional-phase entry (from a PCT) — the 30/31-month deadlines are largely hard cut-offs; miss them and you generally lose the ability to pursue protection in that territory.

The grace period is your safety net — with a cost

Most fee deadlines give you a grace window after the due date, so a single missed date usually isn't instantly fatal — but it's not free. You'll pay a surcharge, and you've burned the buffer. The danger is when a deadline is missed and nobody notices until the grace period has also passed. That's when a valuable right quietly disappears.

Can you get it back? Revival & restoration

If a deadline is missed outright, there may still be a route back:

  • US — petition to revive: an abandoned application or lapsed patent can often be revived on the basis that the delay was "unintentional," with a petition and additional fees. It's discretionary and gets harder the longer you leave it.
  • Europe — further processing / re-establishment of rights: some missed EPO deadlines can be remedied by further processing (with a fee); more serious ones need re-establishment of rights, which has strict conditions and short windows.

None of these are guaranteed, and they cost far more in fees and attorney time than the original deadline would have. Revival is a fire extinguisher, not a plan.

The real cost of a miss

The maths is brutal: the reminder that would have saved a patent costs nothing, while the patent itself can protect a product line worth millions. A single lapsed renewal can hand a competitor the freedom to copy what you spent years and legal fees protecting. That asymmetry — tiny cost to prevent, huge cost to suffer — is exactly why missed deadlines are the most avoidable way a portfolio loses value.

How to make sure it never happens

  1. One source of truth for every US and European case — not a deadline living in each attorney's inbox.
  2. Deadlines pulled from the offices, so a new office action or fee shows up automatically instead of relying on a hand-off.
  3. Reminders well ahead of each date, not the day it's due.
